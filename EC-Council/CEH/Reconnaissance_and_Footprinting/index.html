Module IV

Reconnaissance & Footprinting

Reconnaissance builds the intelligence picture: employees, systems, domains, documents, exposures, and public-facing clues that shape later phases.

Take Module Quiz
Study content
Lesson 1: Recon Basics

Information gathering strategy

Reconnaissance collects environmental knowledge that helps a tester understand likely targets and weak points.

Focus points

  • Employee information may include names, roles, email addresses, phone numbers, and locations.
  • System information includes operating systems, applications, network mappings, and security policies.
  • Physical and operational information can reveal routines, dependencies, and entry points.

Summary

Good reconnaissance reduces guesswork and helps later scanning focus on likely-value targets.

Exam angle

Footprinting tools such as Whois, nslookup, and ARIN are commonly associated with network reconnaissance.

Lesson 2: Countermeasures

Reducing public exposure

Defenders can lower reconnaissance value by minimizing unnecessary exposure and service disclosure.

Focus points

  • Service banners, metadata, and unused services can all leak useful information to attackers.
  • Administrative hardening includes disabling unnecessary services and hiding version details where practical.
  • Public documents and exposed source or directory listings can reveal names, software, and infrastructure.

Summary

Reconnaissance countermeasures do not eliminate exposure completely, but they reduce easy wins for attackers.

Exam angle

Look for answers that limit what an attacker can learn before exploitation begins.

Module focus

Passive and active intelligence gathering before direct exploitation.

Why it matters

Gather the target story before touching the target infrastructure.
