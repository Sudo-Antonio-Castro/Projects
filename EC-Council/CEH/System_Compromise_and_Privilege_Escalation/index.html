Module VIII

System Compromise & Privilege Escalation

System hacking topics include credential attacks, privilege escalation, persistence, and the actions attackers take after gaining initial access.

Take Module Quiz
Study content
Lesson 1: Gaining Access

Passwords and local compromise

Attackers use password attacks, stolen credentials, removable media, and weak host protections to gain access.

Focus points

  • Keyloggers, rainbow tables, John the Ripper, and audit tools all appear in host-compromise scenarios.
  • Physical access can dramatically change what is possible against a workstation or server.
  • Credential weakness often becomes the easiest path into a system.

Summary

Weak passwords and exposed credential material can collapse otherwise strong defensive layers.

Exam angle

If the question involves cracking or recovering passwords, think credential attack tooling and offline analysis.

Lesson 2: Escalate and Persist

Privilege, backdoors, and tracks

After entry, attackers often escalate privileges, maintain access, and remove evidence of activity.

Focus points

  • Privilege escalation may exploit misconfigurations, weak controls, or insecure installation artifacts.
  • Backdoors and remote access methods help preserve long-term access.
  • Clearing logs, hiding files, or concealing processes helps reduce detection.

Summary

Access alone is not the end state—control, persistence, and stealth often follow.

Exam angle

When a scenario mentions long-term unauthorized access, persistence is the core concept.

Module focus

Password attacks, local compromise, persistence, and track covering.

Why it matters

This is where the engagement shifts from discovery to active compromise and control.
