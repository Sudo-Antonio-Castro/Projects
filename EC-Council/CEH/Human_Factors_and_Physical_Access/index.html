Module III

Human Factors & Physical Access

This module examines social engineering tactics, physical attacks, and the practical controls that reduce human and facility risk.

Take Module Quiz
Study content
Lesson 1: Social Engineering

Manipulating trust

Social engineering succeeds by exploiting curiosity, urgency, fear, authority, and routine behavior.

Focus points

  • Phishing and internet-based tricks target users through messages, links, and fraudulent requests.
  • Attackers often gather personal or organizational details first to make a pretext believable.
  • Human-targeted attacks frequently bypass strong technical controls if awareness is weak.

Summary

The most effective social engineering attacks feel plausible because they use real names, routines, and context.

Exam angle

Questions about pretexting, phishing, and manipulation usually hinge on the human trigger being exploited.

Lesson 2: Physical Security

On-site attacks and prevention

Physical access can lead directly to data theft, device compromise, or follow-on network intrusion.

Focus points

  • Physical attacks include tailgating, theft, badge abuse, rogue device placement, and unauthorized media use.
  • Countermeasures include visitor controls, surveillance, barriers, locked rooms, and awareness training.
  • Prevention is strongest when physical and personnel controls support each other.

Summary

Protecting buildings, desks, ports, and people is part of defensive security, not a separate concern.

Exam angle

If an answer strengthens access control at doors, closets, or shared spaces, it may be the best physical-security choice.

Module focus

Social engineering, physical security attacks, and defensive countermeasures.

Why it matters

People and physical spaces are often the easiest path into a secure environment.
