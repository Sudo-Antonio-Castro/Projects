Lesson 1: Foundations

Ethical hacking basics

Ethical hacking uses attacker techniques in a lawful, authorized way to identify weaknesses before malicious actors do.

Focus points

  • Ethical hackers require explicit permission before testing a target system.
  • The CEH-aligned workflow commonly moves through reconnaissance, scanning, gaining access, maintaining access, and covering tracks.
  • A professional tester needs technical depth in operating systems, networking, and security fundamentals.

Summary

Always separate authorized assessment from unauthorized intrusion. Permission is what makes the activity ethical.

Exam angle

If a question contrasts ethical hackers with criminal hackers, the key difference is authorization.

Lesson 2: Lab Mindset

Working like a tester

Practical ethical hacking combines concepts, tools, documentation, and disciplined observation.

Focus points

  • Hands-on work often involves Kali Linux, terminal-based tools, and evidence gathering.
  • Practice questions, demos, and lab simulations reinforce how commands and workflows fit together.
  • Good note-taking helps track commands, findings, and follow-up actions.

Summary

Treat each assessment like an investigation: record what you did, what you found, and what it means.

Exam angle

CEH questions often blend concepts with tool usage, so understand both the idea and the command context.
