Documentation & Ticketing

Good notes make support repeatable and allow clean handoffs.

Core Ideas

  • Record symptoms, affected assets, timestamps, and actions taken.
  • Tie work to ticket IDs, asset tags, and clear ownership.
  • Knowledge bases and SOPs reduce repeated guesswork.

Support Focus

  • Document results, not just actions.
  • Use enough detail for another technician to continue the case.
  • Capture escalation reasons and next steps.
Summary

Documentation protects continuity and accountability.

A+ Exam Tip

Think symptom, scope, steps taken, result, and next action.

Professional Communication

Support quality depends on listening, expectation-setting, and calm delivery.

Core Ideas

  • Use active listening and restate the issue to confirm understanding.
  • Match your wording to the audience and avoid unnecessary jargon.
  • Stay professional in person, by phone, chat, and email.

Support Focus

  • Escalate when authority, time, or expertise runs out.
  • Set clear follow-up expectations.
  • Document promised timelines and handoffs.
Summary

Professional communication reduces repeat contacts and builds trust.

A+ Exam Tip

Acknowledge frustration, confirm the issue, then move into structured troubleshooting.

Operating System Types

A+ expects recognition of desktop, server, mobile, embedded, and appliance operating systems.

Core Ideas

  • Desktop OSs focus on end-user productivity.
  • Server OSs host shared services and centralized management.
  • Mobile OSs emphasize touch, radios, app stores, and power management.

Support Focus

  • Embedded and appliance OSs are optimized for a dedicated function.
  • OS category changes the support workflow and toolset.
  • Identify the platform before selecting a fix path.
Summary

Know what class of system you are supporting before you troubleshoot it.

A+ Exam Tip

Desktop, server, mobile, embedded, and appliance OS types are all fair game.
