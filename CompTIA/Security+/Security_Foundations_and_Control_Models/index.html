Security Concepts

Security begins with protecting confidentiality, integrity, availability, and non-repudiation across systems and data.

Core Ideas

  • Use the CIA triad to evaluate whether a control protects data the right way.
  • Frameworks such as NIST help organizations align security work to business outcomes.

Support Focus

  • Think in terms of identify, protect, detect, respond, and recover.
  • Gap analysis shows where current controls fall short of the target state.
Summary

Security concepts create the language and priorities used throughout the rest of the course.

Security+ Exam Tip

If a scenario asks what a control protects, map it back to confidentiality, integrity, or availability.

Security Controls

Controls can be grouped by how they are implemented and by the job they perform before, during, or after an incident.

Core Ideas

  • Managerial, operational, technical, and physical controls work together.
  • Preventive, detective, and corrective controls are the most common functional types.

Support Focus

  • Directive, deterrent, and compensating controls often appear in policy-heavy questions.
  • Use examples like firewalls, logs, backups, and procedures to classify a control correctly.
Summary

Good answers usually identify both the control category and the control function.

Security+ Exam Tip

Logs are detective, backups are corrective, and policies are directive.
