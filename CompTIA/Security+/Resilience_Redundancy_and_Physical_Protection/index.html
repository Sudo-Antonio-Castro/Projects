Module VII

Resilience depends on knowing what matters, building redundancy, and protecting facilities and people.

This module covers asset management, redundancy strategies, site controls, utilities, recovery planning, and physical security concepts.

Module Content
1 / 3

Asset Management

You cannot protect what you cannot identify, classify, and track throughout its lifecycle.

Core Ideas

  • Hardware, software, data, and service inventories support prioritization and response.
  • Criticality, ownership, and dependency mapping guide backup and recovery planning.

Support Focus

  • Tag assets and keep inventories current enough to support real incidents.
  • Include cloud, mobile, and third-party assets in the inventory picture.
Summary

Asset awareness is a prerequisite for resilience and response.

Security+ Exam Tip

A missing inventory is often the hidden root cause in security and recovery failures.

Redundancy Strategies

Resilience uses duplicate capacity and alternate paths so that one failure does not become a business outage.

Core Ideas

  • High availability, load balancing, RAID, clustering, backups, generators, and diverse links all support uptime.
  • Business continuity and disaster recovery planning should be tested, not assumed.

Support Focus

  • Differentiate fault tolerance from recovery after the fact.
  • Redundancy improves availability but may add cost and complexity.
Summary

Redundancy is about graceful survival, not just restoration after total failure.

Security+ Exam Tip

Backups support recovery; redundant components help prevent outage in the first place.

Physical Security

Physical controls protect facilities, systems, and personnel from theft, tampering, espionage, and environmental disruption.

Core Ideas

  • Locks, barriers, cameras, lighting, guards, sensors, mantraps, and secure areas all serve different roles.
  • Utilities, HVAC, fire suppression, and safety planning are part of site security too.

Support Focus

  • Remember environmental controls along with access controls.
  • Physical access often equals total compromise if critical systems are unprotected.
Summary

Cybersecurity and physical security are tightly connected in real environments.

Security+ Exam Tip

If an attacker can walk up to a system, many purely technical defenses become easier to bypass.
