Threat Actors

Threat analysis starts by evaluating internal versus external actors, sophistication, resources, and motivation.

Core Ideas

  • Nation-states, organized crime, insiders, hacktivists, and unskilled attackers do not behave the same way.
  • Risk is tied to both likelihood and impact, not just the existence of a vulnerability.

Support Focus

  • Financial gain, espionage, disruption, revenge, and ideology all change attacker behavior.
  • Structured campaigns are different from opportunistic attacks.
Summary

Threat actor profiling helps defenders prioritize the most realistic risks first.

Security+ Exam Tip

Insider threats can be malicious or accidental, so do not assume bad intent in every case.

Attack Surfaces

The attack surface includes every place an attacker can interact with software, networks, cloud resources, devices, or users.

Core Ideas

  • Open ports, weak credentials, unpatched software, messaging channels, and supply-chain links all expand risk.
  • Reducing exposure means shrinking access to only what is necessary and then monitoring it.

Support Focus

  • Differentiate a threat vector from the broader attack surface.
  • Watch for lure-based and message-based delivery techniques in scenario questions.
Summary

Attack surface reduction is one of the most practical ways to lower risk.

Security+ Exam Tip

If a service is not needed, do not expose it—especially to the Internet.

Social Engineering

Human behavior is often the easiest route into a secure environment, so attackers rely heavily on deception and urgency.

Core Ideas

  • Impersonation, pretexting, phishing, vishing, smishing, typosquatting, and watering-hole attacks all manipulate trust.
  • Brand impersonation and business email compromise are more targeted and polished than mass phishing.

Support Focus

  • Train users to verify requests through a second channel.
  • Social engineering can be used for reconnaissance as well as direct compromise.
Summary

Many technical attacks only succeed after a user is tricked into helping.

Security+ Exam Tip

Urgency plus authority is a classic red flag for social engineering.
